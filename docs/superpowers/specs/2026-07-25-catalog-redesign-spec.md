## 1. Thesis

**One artifact — a vault — carried across five courses.**

A learner reads our live vault on devnet in the browser (C1), writes its Rust core (C2), wraps that core in Anchor and deploys their own copy (C3), generates a typed client from their own IDL and publishes it to npm plus a dApp that consumes it (C4), then puts a paid tier on it, meters it with x402, lets a budget-capped agent pay for it, and submits a Superteam Earn listing (C5).

Every course consumes the previous course's output as a named, checkable input, and every course ends by naming what it handed forward. The vault was chosen over an invented product because **it is an artifact people already pay for**: Superteam UK and Netherlands ran Anchor Vault and Pinocchio Vault challenges at $300–$600, Superteam Nigeria paid $4,000/62 subs for an Anchor/Pinocchio security template, and Superteam Brasil paid $4,000/12 subs to extend the Solana Vault Standard _programs and SDK_. The through-line terminus is a real listing, not a metaphor.

**Positioning vs Blueshift, in one line:** they own program internals (Pinocchio, assembly, Winternitz, the Token-2022 suite, Mollusk/LiteSVM) and require a local `anchor init` + `.so` upload — we own the graded, credentialed, zero-install path from web2 JavaScript to paid Superteam work, and the two lanes they have no course for at all (Rust-the-language, and x402/agentic stablecoin payments).

Three positioning claims are **retired** because they do not survive contact with evidence:

- ~~"PT-BR is the gap"~~ — Blueshift ships 206/206 pages in genuine pt-BR. **Spanish is the empty lane** (Blueshift es = 0 pages; solana.com PT/ES docs are machine-translated by Lingo.dev, and cookbook/guides have no locale dirs at all).
- ~~"Browser build+deploy is unique"~~ — Solana Playground has done it for years and is the Foundation's own quickstart. The defensible claim is the **wrapper**: sequenced curriculum, hidden-test grading, per-lesson progress, soulbound XP, a credential NFT minted on a successful devnet deploy, and Earn + Turbin3 admission as named termini.
- ~~"We are the beginner option"~~ — Blueshift now ships a "comece do zero" beginner path. Our on-ramp claim is narrower and dateable: **first sequenced, graded, version-stamped `@solana/kit` curriculum**, against a field where the Foundation's courses are de-hosted and frozen at Jan-2025 on `web3.js@1`, freeCodeCamp is hard-pinned to 1.91.8, and our own `solana-fundamentals` has 25 web3.js references and 0 kit references.

**Audience scope, stated explicitly** (reviewer finding, accepted): this catalog serves **segment 1** (web2 JS/TS devs) as primary and **segment 2** (web3 devs deepening) via declared entry points at C3 and C5. **Segment 3 (beginners learning to code) is OUT OF SCOPE for this wave.** Every course gates on working JavaScript. Do not write marketing copy implying otherwise; point segment 3 at freeCodeCamp's JS curriculum and revisit after launch.

### Why each course exists

**1 — `solana-for-web-devs` (8).** The on-ramp gates the whole funnel: ~84% of paid Earn dev work is reachable with TypeScript alone, and every later act assumes a signer and an RPC. Differentiation is dateable — solana.com labels `@solana/web3.js` v1 _and_ `@solana/wallet-adapter-*` superseded, and every free alternative teaches them. Being the first sequenced, graded, version-stamped Kit curriculum is a real claim while it lasts. Lesson 1 reads _our_ deployed reference vault, so the learner uses the product before building it — fixing the structural asymmetry where act one otherwise consumes nothing. Eight, not twelve: the current course spends position 2 on a local Rust/Anchor install (the exact friction the build server exists to remove) and three of its five challenges are mock objects. First executed code lands at lesson 2 instead of lesson 5, and the first completion moment is reachable in one sitting.

**2 — `rust-for-program-devs` (14).** Rust-required listings are only ~16% of paid Earn dev work but carry the best money-per-competitor on the board (~$124 per competing submission, median 14 submissions, vs ~$53 and median 91 for AI-agent bounties). It is the margin play and the gate to everything premium; Turbin3's admission prereq is a Rust transaction. **Blueshift has no Rust-language course across all 27** — they open at `anchor-for-dummies` assuming Rust. Only Cyfrin Updraft contests this rung: video-first, English-only, local toolchain. Fourteen because this is the documented drop-off cliff and today it is our most compressed course — ownership and borrowing get one 355-word lesson, with no lifetimes, traits, generics or modules anywhere. Hard requirement: all 10 current Rust exercises are std-only simulations because grading POSTs to `play.rust-lang.org`, which cannot compile `anchor-lang`. That infrastructure constraint, not authoring taste, produced ten fake Rust exercises. Every exercise here compiles on our build server.

**3 — `building-your-first-solana-program` (15).** This is the artifact Superteam chapters literally pay for, and exactly what Turbin3 admission assumes. Sharpest wedge in the catalog: Blueshift makes you install Rust+Anchor and upload a `.so`; Solana Playground offers browser build+deploy but is a blank IDE with no sequencing, grading, progress or credential. **KEEP, not rewrite** — this is the one existing course that already satisfies all four structural principles: 8 buildable exercises threaded through one cumulative `lib.rs`, a genuinely faded ladder including a real DEBUG rung, and the catalog's only milestone chain. Counter→vault is a rename-level change to a verified exercise ladder. **But it does not stay 16.** The reviewers are right that 16 was inherited from `slots.lock`, not derived from material, and the resulting 4/4/4/4 grid is the exact template artifact principle 1 exists to kill. Re-derived to **15 (3/4/4/4)**: retire the duplicate slot-11 deploy lesson, retire the "deliberately trivial" independent-write in module 1, add the LiteSVM/retrieval pre-flight rung.

**4 — `dapp-and-sdk-with-kit` (11).** Superteam Brasil's two biggest dev bounties are exactly this shape — "Build Solana-native plugins for Zeroclaw" ($5,000 USDG, open) and "Extend the Solana Vault Standard programs & SDK" ($4,000/12 subs) — alongside RPC-reliability SDK ($1,000/19) and validator tooling ($1,000/11), while the $5,000 frontend-only role drew 731 submissions. _Ship the package, not just the page._ Blueshift has no web dApp course at all across 27 slugs, and its `create-your-sdk-with-codama` is 3 lessons of pure codegen mechanics — we don't fight that, we own the wrapper that gets paid (API design, docs, versioning, publish). Codama is the load-bearing joint of the catalog: the IDL published in C3 generates the client imported in C4 and used by the agent in C5, so **C4 literally cannot run without C3's output**.

**5 — `stablecoin-agentic-payments` (9).** The freshest primitive on Solana and the single largest genuine gap in the field. x402 has 37M+ transactions with ~70% of monthly volume on Solana; Visa and Mastercard are x402 Foundation founding members; the audited Subscriptions & Allowances program hit mainnet 2026-06-02 — and within three weeks Superteam Canada paid $2,750 USDG for a code sample (31 subs) and $2,750 for the deep dive (44 subs), ~$100 per competitor, second only to Rust. **Zero course coverage anywhere** — not Blueshift (whose `solana-pay` course is 4 lessons on a four-year-old primitive), not Cyfrin, RareSkills, Ackee or freeCodeCamp; Solana's own coverage is one demo folder. It absorbs AI-agent demand without spending a catalog slot on it: Allowances are the budget primitive for autonomous agents, so agents arrive as a payments capability rather than a framework tour — which matters because agent bounties are the worst odds on Earn. Brazil-framed: BCB Resolution 561 bans stablecoin settlement for regulated cross-border payments from 2026-10-01, so this is framed on domestic merchant acceptance, contractor payouts, subscriptions and agentic budgets, with one lesson teaching the compliance boundary explicitly. **Never ship remittance copy.**

### On-chain implications

**The ordering is not free.** Verified against the live content repo: `solana-fundamentals` is trackId 1 / trackLevel 1 and `building-your-first-solana-program` is trackId 1 / trackLevel 2. Inserting a 14-lesson Rust course between them **collides with the flagship's trackLevel**. There is no in-place edit that achieves the proposed ordering.

Resolution, in order:

1. **Freeze `trackId 1`, `trackLevel 1→5` as the single sequential path.** Today's catalog is five parallel one-course "tracks" (trackIds 1–5) with no through-line. That is the deeper problem the reordering fixes.
2. **Recreate the flagship on DEVNET at trackLevel 3, once, before mainnet.** Devnet was fully reset in July 2026 at ~10 minutes of downtime with all 38 enrollments byte-identical, so this is cheap now and impossible later. `trackId`/`trackLevel` and `creator` are effectively immutable after on-chain creation; the immutability constraint bites at mainnet, not devnet. Batch this with any other catalog reordering.
3. **Create all four new courses with REAL instructor wallets.** All six existing courses were created with the platform authority `B7o8Nf…` as creator (pre-#399), and creator is immutable. This is a known pre-mainnet blocker; do not repeat it. **Prerequisite:** the creator/instructor pipeline is currently broken — `CONTRIBUTING.md` documents an `instructors/` directory that does not exist, no `course.yaml` carries an `instructor` key (all six use `creator:` with a raw pubkey), and `teachers.yaml`'s own header says _"NOTHING READS THIS FILE YET."_ Fix before the first new course goes on-chain.
4. **Retire `solana-fundamentals`, `rust-for-solana`, `anchor-framework`, `solana-frontend`, `defi-on-solana`** rather than editing them. New slugs have unconstrained `slots.lock`; retrofitting a live 12-lesson course requires an `active_lessons` bitmap sync for every insert or delete.
5. **C3 slot arithmetic.** `slots.lock.json` keys are lesson IDs, not slugs. 13 of 16 existing IDs survive rename-only. Two retires (`lesson-bfsp-deploy-to-devnet` slot 11; the module-1 trivial independent-write), one add (`pre-flight-check`) → 16 − 2 + 1 = **15**. Resolve the exact ID map against `slots.lock` before the PR; the principle is _rename freely, retire twice, add once_. `xpPerLesson` stays 20.
6. **xpPerLesson per course**, fixed on-chain, cannot vary by lesson: C1 = 10 (on-ramp tier), C2 = 20, C3 = 20 (unchanged), C4 = 20, C5 = 25. Module milestones, not XP, carry the sense of progression. Adding quiz/`openEnded` blocks costs nothing on-chain because **XP is per-lesson, not per-block** — which is what makes the shape ladder free.

## 4. Full Syllabi

**How to read the tables.** _Shape_ is the guidance rung (worked-example → subgoal-labeled → parsons-completion → independent-write), plus two non-guidance shapes: `concept-retrieval` (spacing checkpoint) and `recap`. _Outcome_ verbs like "deploy-artifact" are orthogonal to guidance — a deploy lesson still declares its guidance rung. _Blocks_ uses only real schema block types: `prose | video | code | quiz | openEnded | wallet-funding | program-explorer | deployed-program-card`. _Skills_ must come from the canonical registry (§5). _Technical anchor_ is the exact API surface an author must hit.

## 5. Ladder & Retrieval Policy

### The shape ladder

Four guidance rungs, faded within a module:

| Rung                   | What the starter contains                                                                   | Copy-resistance under open book                                                                     |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **worked-example**     | Complete, correct, annotated code. Learner runs it, reads it, changes at most one value     | N/A — nothing to copy                                                                               |
| **subgoal-labeled**    | Skeleton with **numbered comment headers** naming each subgoal; 1–2 pre-filled              | Low, deliberately                                                                                   |
| **parsons-completion** | The correct lines **present but scrambled and/or commented out**, plus 1–2 plausible decoys | **High by construction** — the solution is already visible; the work is ordering and discrimination |
| **independent-write**  | Signatures and a written spec with acceptance criteria. No pattern shown inline             | Medium — mitigated by hidden tests using different fixtures                                         |

Plus two non-guidance shapes: **concept-retrieval** (a spacing checkpoint; it does not fade anything and must never be counted as a rung) and **recap** (course close).

**Authoring rules, enforced at review:**

1. **A module may not jump worked-example → independent-write.** At least one intermediate rung must sit between them. _(This was violated in C1 M2 and C4 M2; both fixed above.)_ A `concept-retrieval` lesson in between does **not** satisfy this.
2. **Subgoal labels must appear as literal numbered comments in `starter.rs`/`starter.ts`.** If they exist only in prose, the rung is a worked-example wearing a costume.
3. **Parsons rungs must ship the target code present-but-scrambled, not absent.** "Parsons" with an empty starter is an independent-write.
4. **A module milestone must name a checkable object** — a function signature, a file, a URL, a signature, a PDA address. Capability claims ("you can now…") are not milestones. _(Violated once, in C2 M2; fixed.)_
5. **The last technical rung of a course must be independent-write.** Guidance may fade within a module but must not move backwards across the path — a trackLevel-4 learner who wrote `vault_core.rs` unaided in C2 must not be handed scrambled fragments as their final deliverable. _(Violated in C4 M3; fixed.)_
6. **A 2-lesson capstone module must carry its scaffold inside the capstone lesson** — a completion block followed by an independent block, or a worked exemplar in prose for writing deliverables. Never a single unscaffolded blank page as a module's entire deliverable. _(Applied to C1 M3 and C2 M4; see Appendix A for why C5 M3 is exempt.)_
7. **Length follows material.** No course is authored to a lesson count, and no module is padded to match its siblings. Uneven modules (4/5/3/2 in C2; 3/4/4/4 in C3) are the correct output, not a defect to tidy.

### Entry rungs by segment

The path is a strict prerequisite chain, which is the expertise-reversal configuration (guidance helps novices, _hurts_ experts, d = −0.428) for anyone entering mid-path. Three declared entry points, each with an explicit skip affordance:

| Segment                     | Entry                                                           | Affordance                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1 — web2 JS/TS devs**     | C1 L1                                                           | None needed; the full ladder is for them                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **2 — web3 devs deepening** | **C3 L1** (programs) or **C5 L1** (payments)                    | C3 L1 ships a **known-good `vault_core.rs` reference** for anyone who did not take C2. C5 ships a **reference deployed app + published client** so the payments course is completable standalone. **Both fallbacks are documented, and neither is the default path in the copy.** The credential still requires the learner's own program id / own app URL |
| **3 — learning to code**    | **Out of scope this wave.** Every course gates on working JS/TS | Say so on the catalog page; point at freeCodeCamp's JS curriculum                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |

The C4 dead-end is the sharpest risk: a learner arriving without a deployed program cannot start lesson 1. Publish a **reference vault program id + IDL** as a documented fallback so C4 is completable, but require the learner's own program id for the credential. A hard dead-end at L1 will show up as a completion cliff.

### Where quiz blocks go

`quiz` has been in `schema/lesson.schema.json` since day one, is fully documented in `CONTRIBUTING.md` (stable option ids, `multiSelect`, per-option `feedback`), is demonstrated in `courses/_template/lessons/basics/lesson.yaml`, and is used in **0 of 76 production lessons**. It needs zero engineering and costs zero XP (XP is per-lesson, not per-block).

Placement rules:

- **Every code-bearing lesson carries a quiz** unless it is a pure deploy/fund step.
- **Question style: predict-the-error, predict-the-cost, predict-the-behaviour.** "This code does not compile — which line does the borrow checker reject?" is retrieval practice. "What does `&mut` mean?" is not.
- **Every module ends within one lesson of a retrieval opportunity.**
- **Every course carries exactly one dedicated `concept-retrieval` lesson**, placed where the material stops being additive: C1 L5 (`kit-or-legacy`), C2 L9 (`ownership-checkpoint`), C3 L12 (`pre-flight-check`), C4 L6 (`signers-chains-and-the-legacy-seam`), C5 L2 (`token-or-token-2022`).
- **Every recap carries a cumulative quiz with ≥1 item per skill tag used in the course.** _(C3's recap had none — fixed.)_
- **Cross-course seeding, mandatory:** every retrieval lesson and every recap after C1 draws **at least 2 items from the previous course's skill tags**. This is the only mechanism that exercises the review queue across the path rather than inside a single course, and it costs nothing.
- **Author per-option `feedback` strings.** The schema supports it; nothing in production uses it.
- `openEnded` is never graded, awards no XP and gates nothing. Use it for self-explanation and for collecting URLs the platform cannot yet verify — never as a substitute for a graded rung.

**Do not claim quizzes gate XP.** They do not, on today's platform. If per-lesson XP gating on retrieval is wanted, that is a platform change (§7), not an authoring decision.

### Skill tags and the review queue

**A canonical registry already exists and the syllabi ignored it.** `skills.yaml` at the content repo root holds 24 slugs: `solana-fundamentals, account-model, transactions, keypairs-wallets, rpc, rust, borsh-serialization, solana-programs, anchor, pdas, cpi, program-testing, program-deployment, spl-tokens, defi, amm, lending, staking, oracles, wallet-adapter, react, web3-frontend, siws, typescript`. It is **not referenced by `lesson.schema.json`** (which declares only `id`, `slug`, `title`, `blocks`) and **not enforced by `content-lint`**, so today `skills:` is unvalidated free text.

The draft syllabi invented ~130 distinct tags across 57 lessons, mostly singletons, with PDAs alone fragmented four ways (`pdas` / `pda-derivation` / `pda` / `pda-signing`). A fixed 1/3/7/21 Leitner queue keyed on that vocabulary degenerates into "redo that lesson," and the named interleaving target (PDA vs ATA, CPI account lists, Anchor constraints) is un-buildable.

**Policy:**

1. **Tags come from `skills.yaml`. Adding a tag means a PR to `skills.yaml`, reviewed as a vocabulary decision.** No free-text tags in lesson files.
2. **`content-lint` gains a gate: every `skills:` entry must resolve to a `skills.yaml` slug.** This is the cheapest platform change on the list and it is what makes the rest enforceable.
3. **Registry migration for this catalog** — retire `wallet-adapter` (superseded stack), `defi`/`amm`/`lending`/`staking`/`oracles` (retired course), `rpc` → `rpc-reads`; add: `solana-kit`, `wallet-standard`, `signers`, `transaction-fees`, `compute-budget`, `priority-fees`, `simulation`, `confirmation`, `error-handling`, `debugging`, `explorer-reading`, `api-currency`, `rust-types`, `rust-ownership`, `rust-borrowing`, `rust-lifetimes`, `rust-structs`, `rust-enums`, `rust-traits`, `rust-option`, `rust-result`, `rust-control-flow`, `rust-modules`, `rust-move-semantics`, `checked-arithmetic`, `compiler-errors`, `build-server`, `toolchain-versions`, `account-layout`, `rent-and-space`, `program-errors`, `program-security`, `program-build`, `idl`, `codama`, `client-codegen`, `api-design`, `npm-publishing`, `semver`, `ci`, `deployment`, `technical-writing`, `version-pinning`, `solana-explorer`, `token-2022`, `transfer-hook`, `payment-model-selection`, `solana-pay`, `x402-protocol`, `api-monetization`, `protocol-versioning`, `subscription-authority`, `fixed-delegation`, `recurring-delegation`, `subscriptions-program`, `agent-budgets`, `brazil-compliance`, `earn-submission`. Roughly 80 total.
4. **Minimum reuse bar: every tag must appear on ≥2 lessons across the catalog**, or it is merged into a broader tag. A tag with one lesson behind it cannot generate a review item — it can only re-serve the lesson. Exception: `brazil-compliance` and `earn-submission` are single-use by design and are review-exempt; mark them as such in `skills.yaml`.
5. **No tag may be so broad it carries no signal.** `typescript`, `react` and `program-basics` are dropped as review keys (they may stay as catalog-page facets).
6. **Interleaving pairs the queue must be able to build**, and which therefore must share vocabulary: `pdas` ↔ `account-model` (PDA vs ATA vs wallet), `cpi` ↔ `signers` (who signs what), `anchor` ↔ `program-security` (which constraint blocks which attack), `checked-arithmetic` ↔ `rust-result` (fail how), `compute-budget` ↔ `transaction-fees` (requested vs consumed).

## 8. Open Decisions for the Owner

1. **PB-1: build a Kit-capable sandbox, or accept pure-function grading permanently?** This spec assumes the fallback and is authorable today. Building the real thing (browser-safe Kit bundle + crypto shim in the isolate) is the only path to a graded lesson where the learner's code actually talks to devnet. It is the largest single platform investment in this plan and it gates the front door of the catalog. **Decision needed before C1 authoring starts, not before it finishes.**
2. **PB-3b: is `cargo test --lib` a launch blocker for C2, or does C2 ship with compile-only grading and softened copy?** Under compile-only, a learner whose `withdraw` calls `checked_add` passes the capstone. The spec ships the verification-harness mitigation, but the honest artifact claim changes either way.
3. **Do the three missing capabilities ship (signed-transaction / source-file / published-artifact), or do C1, C2 and C4 collect artifacts via `openEnded` and soften their milestone copy?** Recommendation: ship `published-artifact` only (it covers C1 L7 and C4 L4/L10), accept `openEnded` for the rest. Milestones that cannot be verified are not milestones — but the copy can be honest cheaply.
4. **Is C5 pulled to Wave 1 and published standalone before C4?** It is the only course with zero platform blockers, the only global first-mover position we hold, and its material has a live decay clock. Cost: it publishes with a reference-app fallback rather than the learner's own C4 app, which weakens the through-line for early cohorts.
5. **Is Spanish promoted to a launch language for C2/C4/C5, or does PT-BR stay first everywhere?** The evidence says ES is the empty lane and PT-BR is table stakes against Blueshift. The brand says Brazil-first. This is a positioning call, not a research question.
6. **Segment 3 (beginners learning to code): confirmed out of scope for this wave?** The spec states it in writing. If that is wrong, it is a sixth course, not an adjustment to C1.

## Appendix A — Review Findings Rejected or Rescoped

Every high and medium finding from both reviews was applied except the following. Applied fixes are marked inline throughout §4 and §5.

**A1 — "The JS mock SDK would make C1 L3 grade green while teaching the opposite." (Reviewer 1, high) — ACCEPTED, MECHANISM CORRECTED.**
The defect is real and the mock must go (PB-6): `findProgramAddressSync` returns a fabricated hash with bump hardcoded to 254 and `isOnCurve` unconditionally returns `true` — the JavaScript twin of the `DefaultHasher` model we delete from the Rust side. But the stated failure mode is wrong. The mock is a `@solana/web3.js` v1 shim; a Kit-based lesson cannot resolve `@solana/kit` in the isolate **at all**, so C1 L3 would **fail closed**, not silently grade green. This matters for sequencing: the mock retirement is _cleanup_ that rides along with retiring the legacy exercises, while PB-1 is the actual blocker. Treating them as one item would have put cleanup on the critical path.

**A2 — "Treat the capability enum as a catalog-wide launch blocker." (Reviewer 2, high) — RESCOPED.**
Rejected as stated. C3's entire milestone chain is `wallet-funding → funded-wallet → deployable → deployed-program → deployed-program-card`, all of which exist and ship today — it is the one course whose milestones are fully verified on the current platform. C5 uses `openEnded` by design, correctly, because a submitted Earn listing is not machine-verifiable in principle. Declaring a catalog-wide blocker would gate the credential-producing course on work it does not need. Correctly scoped: **C1, C2 and C4 only** (PB-2).

**A3 — "C4 lessons 3 and 4 have no authorable language; ship them as prose + quiz." (Reviewer 1, medium) — HALF REJECTED.**
The premise is right: `language` is `["typescript","rust"]`, so package.json and GitHub Actions YAML are not authorable as graded artifacts. The recommended remedy is wrong for L3. The reviewer argues a TS block over a package.json "would be exactly the mock-object busywork the audit condemned in `account-challenge`" — but `account-challenge` was condemned because its tests _echoed its own input_ (`createMockAccount()` returning an object literal, tests asserting field presence). A function `classifyChange(before: Idl, after: Idl): 'major'|'minor'|'patch'`, graded against hidden IDL-diff fixtures, has a right answer that is not derivable from the input by echo, and hardcoding fails. That is a legitimate graded rung and it is the single most transferable skill in the module. **L3 keeps a graded block; L4's YAML does ship as prose.**

**A4 — "Three courses end in a 2-lesson capstone module where the deliverable rests on one unscaffolded lesson; split them." (Reviewer 2, medium) — ACCEPTED FOR C1 AND C2, REJECTED FOR C5.**
Applied to C1 M3 and C2 M4 via an in-lesson fade (a completion block preceding the independent block) rather than by adding lessons — which preserves the length-follows-material principle the same review defends elsewhere. Rejected for C5 M3: the cited evidence is about **project-based learning needing milestone scaffolding**, which is a claim about construction tasks. L8's deliverable is a piece of **writing**. The right scaffold for writing is a worked exemplar of the target shape, which the lesson already carries in prose ("problem → minimal reference implementation → the three semantics that bite → runnable repro with exact pins → dated version banner"). Adding a construction rung before a writing task would be scaffolding the wrong skill.

**A5 — "Every module opens at worked-example inside a strict linear chain — this is the expertise-reversal configuration for segment 2 across the whole path." (Reviewer 2, high) — RESCOPED.**
The concern is valid; the scope is not. Segment 2 was never routed through C1 — the catalog declares C3 as their entry point, and the mismatch the reviewer describes ("49 lessons of material they know") only arises if you assume they start at lesson 1, which nothing asks them to do. The genuine residue is that **C5 had no independent entry** for someone who wants payments only and does not care about writing an Anchor program. Fixed narrowly in §5: documented reference fallbacks at C3 L1 and C5 L1, with the credential still requiring the learner's own artifact. A placement test across five courses would be a large platform build to solve a problem two paragraphs of routing copy solve.

**A6 — "C4 module 3 never reaches independent-write; promote L10 **or** add an eleventh rung." (Reviewer 2, medium) — ACCEPTED, ONE HALF REJECTED.**
L10 is promoted to independent-write (the state machine is specified, not supplied). The alternative — adding a twelfth lesson — is rejected: C4's length was derived from material, and the reviewer's own strongest finding is that C3 drifted precisely because a count was chosen before the material was. The fix is the guidance rung, not the lesson count.

**A7 — "C4 L5's install list omits `swr` and `@tanstack/react-query`." (Reviewer 1, low) — ACCEPTED AS A VERIFY-AT-AUTHORING ITEM, NOT AS FACT.**
